Measuring coordinated information operations during a live democratic event — defensively, transparently, and at platform scale.

The 2026 Brazilian general election culminates in a runoff on October 25, preceded by a legally mandated campaign blackout beginning October 24. This program develops and validates a read-only detection methodology for the documented operation classes of this election cycle — counterfeit official broadcasts, coordinated rumor cascades, synthetic impersonation, and paid content delivered during the blackout — using the Meta Content Library and API within the Meta Secure Research Environment.

About the program

Brazil's Superior Electoral Court (TSE) treats coordinated disinformation as a first-order threat to the 2026 election. The 2024 municipal elections and the 2026 first round documented a stable repertoire of operations: cloned candidate videos, counterfeit "official results" livestreams monetized through Pix payment scams, coordinated rumor cascades, and synthetic narratives amplified by inauthentic engagement. Facebook and Instagram are among the most-used platforms in Brazil, yet independent measurement of these operations during the legally sensitive blackout window remains difficult.

This research program closes that measurement gap. It pairs large-scale lawful access to public content (via the Meta Content Library and API) with transparent, auditable detectors adapted from Vigia, the applicant's prototype monitoring system, producing validated detection methods that civil-society monitors in other democracies can reuse.

Research questions

  1. Prevalence. What is the measured volume and growth of election-integrity-relevant public content (false-results claims, impersonation of official bodies, vote-buying solicitation, incitement) during the blackout-and-runoff window, compared to a baseline period?
  2. Coordination. To what extent do documented operation classes exhibit cross-page and cross-platform coordination structures — repeated content, synchronized timing, shared link domains — visible in public content metadata?
  3. Paid surface. What volume of paid political advertising is delivered during the legally mandated blackout — itself a TSE violation — and how is it distributed across advertisers and regions?
  4. Method. What precision and detection latency do transparent rule-based detectors achieve against researcher-verified ground truth during a live event, and how should severity thresholds be calibrated for operational monitoring?

Methods

Quantitative content analysis with computational detection, conducted entirely inside the Meta Secure Research Environment. Observation spans a baseline week and the event window (blackout eve through certification), extending into post-event weeks for lifecycle measurement.

  • Collection. Read-only Content Library API queries: Portuguese-language keyword and hashtag panels (fraude, apuração, resultado oficial, candidate and institution names), target panels of public pages and groups documented by Brazilian fact-checkers and the TSE, and Ad Library queries for paid content — all within the 500k items / 7-day API budget.
  • Detection. Transparent detectors: near-duplicate text clustering, coordinated-timing cascades, impersonation heuristics, audience-anomaly signals. No generative models; classifiers are read-only and every verdict is inspectable.
  • Ground truth. Stratified samples of flagged clusters are independently coded against a published codebook; precision and detection latency are reported per detector class.
  • Analysis. Volume curves by class and window; network analysis of cross-page content reuse; advertiser concentration; detector evaluation.

Data protection & ethics

  • Read-only charter. No posting, no account interaction, no deanonymization of private individuals. Analysis is restricted to public content and platform-provided metadata.
  • Enclave discipline. All queries execute inside the Meta Secure Research Environment; only aggregate results leave the enclave. No raw post content is downloaded to local infrastructure.
  • Human subjects. Aggregate-level analysis of public content; no individual profiling. IRB guidance is being sought through UC Berkeley's Office for the Protection of Human Subjects.
  • Legal context. Research use complies with Brazil's LGPD as applied to publicly available information for research purposes (Art. 7, XI; Art. 11, §2º, III).
  • Outputs. Open working papers, a methods paper on detector validation, and aggregated artifacts shared with Brazilian fact-checking organizations (Aos Fatos, Comprova, Lupa).
